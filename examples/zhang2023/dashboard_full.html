About this run

Full coding (condition LJA): the same three coders (coder 1 Claude Opus 5, coder 2 GPT-5.6, coder 3 Gemini Flash 3.7) coded every thread with the codes the decision model (TypeSafe Jev) screened for it; then an adversary from another family challenged each coder's batch and the coder answered, keeping or revising each assignment. Agreement after the adversaries is agreement among coder-adversary pipelines. The Lite row of the table below is the same codebook coded without screening or adversaries.

The codebook (version 1.2, 72 codes across five research questions) came out of discovery by three analysts from the same three families, reconciliation by Claude Opus 5 with a GPT adversary, author review, and one consolidation round in which codes the coders could not apply consistently were merged, relabelled or dropped. The research questions are Zhang et al.'s own: functions implemented with Copilot, purposes of using it, benefits, limitations and challenges, and expected features.

The paper reports a median alpha of 0.77 for Lite and 0.87 for Full on the evaluation threads; this page recomputes them from the coders' assignments: 0.87 here.

T01 Keeping Copilot on as the completer while I type, in whatever editor and beside whatever completion list I have (RQ1.5) · alpha 0.59 · any coder 30, consensus 13, unanimous 10
Definition
A purpose code: the poster uses, recommends or arranges to have Copilot complete the line, expression, function body or text entry they are already typing, including using it as the completion engine next to or in place of the IDE's own member list. The test is that the text reports or recommends this use - not that a completion worked, and not that something about it failed.
Include
Statements that Copilot suggests or completes code, markup, README text or string entries while typing; accepting, triggering, navigating or cycling inline suggestions, including opening the panel of candidate solutions and choosing among them; recommendations of Copilot as the AI autocompletion to use; choosing or changing an IDE, version or workflow so its suggestions are available where the poster works; and statements assigning Copilot and the IDE's own completer (IntelliSense, Intelephense, a language server, Tabnine) their roles in the poster's own typing, including Copilot being the completion they still rely on.
Exclude
How much of one offered suggestion to insert goes to T68; a comment, name or pseudocode written as the prompt goes to T36; wanting suggestions silenced or on demand goes to T15; a request that ghost text and the editor's completion list coexist, be told apart or be prioritised goes to T19 (expected feature); a report that the ghost text suppressed or outran the member list, or that a suggested member exists nowhere, goes to T14 or T05 (limitations); a verdict ranking Copilot against IntelliSense or a rival goes to T52 (benefit); a statement that the completion delivered goes to T21; asking it to explain or document existing code goes to T26; reproducing Copilot's grey inline text in one's own extension goes to T62; Copilot missing from an IDE, platform or language goes to T04, T59 or T12.

T05 The suggestion was wrong, invented or of no use (RQ2.2) · alpha 0.44 · any coder 17, consensus 6, unanimous 3
Definition
A limitation: the poster or an answerer reports that what Copilot produced is faulty in content - incorrect, inefficient, covering only part of the task, naming an API, property, option or constant that does not exist, or giving nothing usable when asked about the poster's own bug. The test is a statement in the text that the output was wrong or useless.
Include
Reports that a Copilot snippet, import, URL, value or option was broken, invented, inefficient or undocumented-and-wrong; accounts of a plausible suggestion accepted on Tab and debugged hours later; statements that Copilot was tried on this problem and produced nothing usable while humans are asked instead.
Exclude
A suggestion whose shape was right and whose single invented or subtly wrong detail is the point, framed as 'it looked fine', goes to T51; no suggestion arriving at all goes to T02, and output that arrives stunted, repeated or unclosed to T70; code the poster cannot judge or read goes to T06; wanting only part of a correct block goes to T29; Labs, Chat and brush failures go to T69; silence confined to one language goes to T59; the expectation that suggestions be correct and use real identifiers goes to T44 (expected feature); the poster's own checking practice goes to T03 (purpose).
